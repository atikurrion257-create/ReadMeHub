1Password vs Bitwarden (2026): The Verdict, Verified Prices, and Who Should Buy Which

Quick verdict

Buy 1Password if a refined, effortless experience for you or your family justifies paying roughly $28–47 more per year than Bitwarden Premium. Buy Bitwarden if you lead with value, want a genuinely unlimited free tier, or require open source and self-hosting. Both use zero-knowledge encryption and are safe, reputable choices — this is a values decision, not a safety decision.

Best for 1Password

Households and professionals who want the most polished apps, best family sharing UX, and features like Travel Mode — and consider experience worth $3.99/mo (verified regular price; $2.99 first-year promo).

Best for Bitwarden

Budget-conscious users (including 'free'), open-source advocates, self-hosters, and families who want 6 premium accounts for $3.99/mo total ($0.67/person at capacity).

Choose 1Password if…

  • You'll pay for polish: 1Password's apps, autofill, and organization are the category benchmark
  • Family sharing is central — unlimited shared vaults with the smoothest admin and recovery UX
  • You want Watchtower's proactive security guidance and Travel Mode
  • You're a new customer — the verified first-year promo ($2.99 Individual / $4.49 Families) softens year one
  • You prefer not to think about billing terms: one annual plan, transparent regular pricing

Choose Bitwarden if…

  • Price is the constraint: free forever, or $19.80/yr for Premium (both verified)
  • You want open-source code, self-hosting, or a US/EU-hosted, audit-friendly stack
  • Your household is up to 6 people — Families covers 6 premium accounts at $3.99/mo total
  • You want an integrated TOTP authenticator without paying 1Password prices
  • You're advising a mixed group: Bitwarden's free tier means nobody is priced out of basic security

The key difference

You are choosing between experience and leverage. 1Password sells the best-finished product in the category at $47.88/yr (Individual, regular). Bitwarden gives the security fundamentals away free and sells convenience for $19.80/yr. Neither has a meaningful security-quality advantage for typical users; the difference is app polish, family UX, business tooling — and price.

The tiebreaker

If both fit your budget and needs, let two questions decide. (1) Who is this for? Families who want zero friction → 1Password; mixed groups or budget-shared households → Bitwarden. (2) What annoys you more: paying ~$28/yr more for a better product, or occasionally clunkier software? Your honest answer is the verdict. You can also run both trials — 1Password's 14-day trial and Bitwarden's free tier make this the rare decision you can test before making.

FAQ

Is 1Password safer than Bitwarden?
Not meaningfully for typical users. Both use zero-knowledge AES-256 encryption. 1Password adds a Secret Key (a second secret), Bitwarden offers open-source verifiability and self-hosting. Choose on experience and price, not safety.
Can I switch from one to the other easily?
Yes — both support standard CSV export/import, and Bitwarden documents direct imports from 1Password. Budget an hour, change your master password everywhere after moving, and keep the old export encrypted while you verify.
Which is better for families?
For experience: 1Password (unlimited shared vaults, smooth recovery). For value: Bitwarden (6 premium accounts for $3.99/mo total). The full per-person math is in our families use-case page.
Which has the better free version?
Bitwarden — 1Password has no free tier. Bitwarden Free is unlimited in passwords and devices, verified on the official pricing page in September 2026.
